# Safety Framework

## Primary Safety Principle

> No submission without stabilization.

In altered gravity, joint locks and chokes can become dangerous if bodies are rotating unpredictably. Submissions should only be legal after controlled stabilization of the relevant limb, spine-line, and shared rotation.

## Core Risks

- cervical spine torque
- uncontrolled wall impact
- rotational disorientation
- joint overextension
- panic response
- motion sickness
- grip/tether entanglement
- breathing restriction
- equipment malfunction
- delayed referee intervention

## Likely Rule Restrictions

Early versions should restrict or ban:

- twisting neck locks
- uncontrolled spinal rotation
- high-speed wall impacts
- blind rotational throws
- submissions during uncontrolled tumble
- strikes
- hard suit-handle yanks near the neck
- uncontrolled tether wrapping

## Safety Systems to Explore

- padded spherical or rounded arena
- emergency stabilization rails
- referee override tether
- verbal tap
- physical tap
- wearable sensor tap
- biometric monitoring
- velocity limits
- torque monitoring
- motion-capture foul detection
- soft-grip suit design
- automatic reset protocols

## Referee Role

The referee should not only judge scoring. The referee must monitor:

- unsafe velocity
- uncontrolled rotation
- neck exposure
- tether hazards
- delayed tap
- panic/freeze response
- wall-impact risk
- equipment failure

## Design Philosophy

Zero Gravity Grappling should be designed as a sport of technical control, not reckless spectacle.
