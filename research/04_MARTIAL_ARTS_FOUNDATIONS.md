# Martial Arts Foundations

Zero Gravity Grappling should not be framed as merely Brazilian jiu-jitsu in space. It should draw from the wider grappling and movement world.

## Core Lineages and Analogs

- Brazilian jiu-jitsu
- Japanese jūjutsu
- judo
- wrestling
- sambo
- catch wrestling
- aikido and redirection arts
- contact improvisation
- partner acrobatics
- aerial arts
- underwater rescue and body control
- astronaut EVA movement
- confined-space stabilization

## Japanese Concepts to Preserve and Transform

### 柔術 / Jūjutsu

The art of yielding, adaptability, and softness overcoming force.

In altered gravity, yielding becomes central because reckless force destabilizes both athletes.

### 崩し / Kuzushi

Traditionally, off-balancing.

In altered gravity, kuzushi becomes disruption of usable orientation, rotational control, and recovery pathways.

### 間合い / Maai

Distance, interval, timing, and relational space.

In altered gravity, maai becomes spherical and three-dimensional.

### 重心 / Jūshin

Center of gravity.

In altered gravity, this becomes center of mass and shared center-of-mass management.

### 体捌き / Tai Sabaki

Body movement or body management.

In altered gravity, this becomes three-dimensional reorientation.

### 受け身 / Ukemi

Receiving a fall.

In altered gravity, ukemi becomes receiving motion, impact, drift, wall contact, and rotational force.

## Key Transformation

Traditional jiu-jitsu positions are partly ground-dependent. Zero Gravity Grappling must ask:

- What survives without the floor?
- What transforms?
- What becomes obsolete?
- What new positions become possible?
