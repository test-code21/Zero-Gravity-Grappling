# Source Provenance

## Purpose

This file defines how the research program should preserve where claims, documents, and conclusions came from.

## Current Corpus

The initial repository was bootstrapped from an existing research corpus containing:

- project brief
- core thesis
- deep research prompt
- scientific foundations
- martial arts foundations
- positional atlas
- safety framework
- training pathway
- business and culture notes
- visualization and AI animation plan
- research gaps
- terms and core lines
- multiple long-form deep-research reports

The concise research files in this repository represent the current conceptual foundation. The long-form reports are historical research inputs and should be treated as archive material until their citations and claims are normalized.

## Provenance Rule

When a research mission changes current truth, preserve:

- source title
- author or issuing organization when known
- publication date when known
- URL or stable identifier when available
- access date for web material when relevant
- the exact proposition the source supports
- whether the project is quoting, paraphrasing, or inferring
- confidence and limitations

## Historical Research Reports

Some early deep-research reports contain internal citation markers inherited from the research environment in which they were produced.

Those markers should **not** be treated as durable public citations by themselves.

Before a historical report is promoted from archive material into current doctrine:

1. resolve important citation markers to durable public sources where possible
2. verify that the source actually supports the claim
3. separate source-supported statements from inference
4. record unresolved provenance gaps
5. retain the original report unchanged in the archive

## No Silent Rewriting

Historical documents should be preserved as historical evidence.

If a later review finds:

- an incorrect claim
- an overstatement
- a citation mismatch
- outdated science
- weak inference

do not quietly edit history to make it look correct.

Instead:

- preserve the original
- document the correction
- update current truth
- explain the reason for the change

This allows the repository to show the actual evolution of the research program.
