# Visualization and AI Animation Plan

## Principle

Codify first. Visualize second. Animate third.

If visuals come before mechanics, the concept risks becoming shallow sci-fi spectacle. If mechanics come first, visuals can make the discipline legible.

## Visualization Layers

### Layer 1: Position Cards

Each card should include:

- position name
- objective
- contact points
- controlled axes
- center-of-mass relationship
- common entries
- common escapes
- safety risks
- scoring value
- diagram

### Layer 2: Keyframe Sequences

Create 4–8 frame instructional sequences:

1. starting state
2. grip establishment
3. rotational or positional transition
4. control confirmation
5. escape or finish

### Layer 3: Motion Studies

Simple 3D animations showing:

- action/reaction
- center of mass
- rotation axes
- wall contact
- spin dampening
- tether dynamics

### Layer 4: Sensor Overlay Clips

Educational overlays showing:

- force vectors
- velocity
- torque risk
- grip points
- center of mass
- danger zones
- scoring events

### Layer 5: Cinematic Trailers

Only after the mechanics are coherent.

## Tools to Explore

- Blender
- Unreal Engine
- Unity
- motion capture
- pose estimation
- AI image generation
- AI video generation
- VR/AR prototype environments
- physics simulation tools

## AI Prompt Template

Create a technical visualization of [POSITION NAME] in Zero Gravity Grappling. Show two athletes in a padded spherical altered-gravity training chamber. Emphasize body orientation, contact points, center-of-mass markers, rotation axis, and safety zones. Style should be clean, scientific, credible, and instructional — like a biomechanics diagram meets a martial arts manual. Avoid exaggerated sci-fi combat or violent spectacle.
