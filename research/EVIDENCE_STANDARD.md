# Evidence and Claim Standard

Zero Gravity Grappling is intentionally interdisciplinary. That makes provenance and claim discipline essential.

## Claim Classes

Every consequential claim should be classifiable as one of the following.

### E1 — Established Science

A claim directly supported by credible technical, scholarly, or primary-source evidence.

Examples may include:

- conservation laws
- documented microgravity sensorimotor effects
- published injury or physiology findings
- established spacecraft restraint practices

Requirements:

- cite the source
- preserve the source's actual scope
- do not generalize beyond what the evidence supports

### E2 — Interdisciplinary Inference

A reasoned extension from established evidence or a neighboring discipline.

Examples may include:

- adapting grappling control concepts to a two-body free-space system
- inferring useful lessons from EVA restraint practice
- translating contact-improvisation principles into partner-control hypotheses

Requirements:

- cite the supporting evidence
- state the inferential step
- identify what would falsify the inference

### E3 — Design Proposal

A proposed rule, position, curriculum element, apparatus concept, scoring system, or experimental method.

Examples may include:

- stabilization classes
- a wall-control scoring rule
- a proposed training progression
- a simulated arena design

Requirements:

- never present as validated
- state the problem it is intended to solve
- identify a plausible validation path

### E4 — Speculative Concept

A future-facing possibility whose feasibility is not yet established.

Examples may include:

- an orbital league
- a dedicated commercial-station dojo module
- future cultural traditions around altered-gravity sport

Requirements:

- label clearly
- avoid converting narrative plausibility into technical certainty

### E5 — Experimental Observation

A result directly observed in a documented Zero Gravity Grappling experiment, simulation, or test.

Requirements:

- preserve protocol
- preserve conditions and instrumentation
- preserve negative results
- distinguish observation from interpretation
- record replication status

## Confidence

Claim class and confidence are different.

A claim may be:

- high-confidence E2 inference
- low-confidence E1 interpretation because the source is weak
- high-confidence E5 observation with limited external validity

Recommended confidence labels:

- **High**
- **Moderate**
- **Low**
- **Unknown**

Every high-consequence safety or scientific claim should explain why its confidence level is warranted.

## Source Hierarchy

Prefer, when available:

1. primary technical standards and agency documentation
2. peer-reviewed research
3. authoritative technical manuals
4. systematic reviews and high-quality secondary syntheses
5. expert commentary
6. informal analogs and community reports

Lower-ranked evidence can still be useful, especially for generating hypotheses, but should not silently inherit the authority of higher-ranked sources.

## Contradictions

When sources conflict:

1. preserve the conflict
2. compare scope, population, environment, and methodology
3. avoid forcing premature reconciliation
4. identify what further evidence would resolve the disagreement

Conflicting evidence is a research result, not a documentation problem.

## Research Discipline

The program should reward:

- discovering that an attractive idea is wrong
- reducing uncertainty
- improving source quality
- narrowing a claim
- replacing a metaphor with a measurable variable
- documenting what remains unknown

The program should not reward concept proliferation for its own sake.
