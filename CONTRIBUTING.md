# Contributing

Zero Gravity Grappling is a public research program. Useful critique, sources, experiments, simulations, and interdisciplinary perspectives are welcome.

## What Helps Most

High-value contributions tend to do one of the following:

- identify a false assumption
- provide a stronger primary or technical source
- distinguish established science from inference
- expose a safety problem
- test a positional claim
- improve a physics or biomechanics model
- document an analog from another discipline
- make an experiment cheaper, safer, or more informative
- explain why a proposed concept does **not** work

## Evidence Labels

When making substantive claims, identify the basis where possible:

- **Established science**
- **Interdisciplinary inference**
- **Design proposal**
- **Speculative concept**
- **Experimental observation**

Do not present a design proposal as validated science.

## Safety

Do not treat repository concepts as instructions for unsupervised physical experimentation.

Proposed human-subject or high-risk physical tests should include explicit safety constraints and should not be represented as validated merely because they are plausible.

## IP Publication Gate

Do not intentionally publish enabling technical details for a potentially valuable novel invention without first checking the repository's [IP Publication Gate](governance/IP_PUBLICATION_GATE.md).

Ordinary scientific discussion, theory, critique, and research remain public by default.

## Style

Prefer:

- precise language
- falsifiable claims
- explicit uncertainty
- good sources
- small bounded experiments
- preservation of negative results

Avoid:

- “MMA in space” framing
- exaggerated certainty
- sci-fi spectacle presented as engineering
- needless terminology proliferation
- protecting an idea from criticism because it sounds cool
