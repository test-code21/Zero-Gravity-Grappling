# Training Pathway

## Development Principle

Begin with present-day altered-gravity platforms and analogs. Grow toward orbital sport as infrastructure matures.

## Phase 1: Earth-Based Movement Preparation

Training tools:

- jiu-jitsu positional drills
- wrestling hand fighting
- gymnastics rings
- climbing/grip training
- wall drills
- breathwork
- vestibular conditioning
- partner acrobatics
- contact improvisation

## Phase 2: Analog Training

Training environments:

- underwater neutral-buoyancy-style practice
- suspension rigs
- aerial harness systems
- bungee-supported movement
- padded wall chambers
- VR/AR orientation drills
- motion-capture movement analysis

## Phase 3: Parabolic Flight Micro-Drills

Commercial or research parabolic flights could enable short microgravity drill windows.

Early drills should focus on:

- orientation recovery
- safe partner contact
- grip-and-release protocols
- floating guard experiments
- spin dampening
- wall contact recovery
- two-person stabilization
- rescue/body-control simulations

## Phase 4: Demonstration Events and Research Community

Build a multidisciplinary community involving:

- martial artists
- astronauts
- aerospace medicine researchers
- biomechanists
- physical therapists
- dancers
- divers
- acrobats
- VR designers
- human factors researchers

## Phase 5: Orbital Module

Long-term goal:

> a dedicated altered-gravity sport/research module inside a commercial station or orbital habitat.

## Human Meaning

The first space sport may not begin as spectacle. It may begin as humans learning how to touch, stabilize, and trust each other in a new environment.
